# Financial literacy education games — comparable products

> Researched 2026-08-05. Goal: find HUSTLE's closest cousins — products aimed at teaching
> real financial/business decision-making to students, not entertainment-first tycoon games —
> and see what they get right that HUSTLE currently doesn't.

## EVERFI (FutureSmart, The Stock Market Game)

School-deployed financial literacy platform, free to schools (sponsor-funded). Closest
existing comparable to HUSTLE's actual context (facilitated classroom deployment, NQF-style
standards alignment).

- **FutureSmart** puts middle-schoolers in a role (city mayor improving a community's
  financial health) rather than a blank-slate player — same move HUSTLE already makes with
  the archetype pick, just followed through further.
- **The Stock Market Game** gives virtual money tied to real market activity, letting students
  "make bold choices or play it safe without real consequences" — low-stakes experimentation
  as a deliberate design choice, not a limitation.
- Courses align to Jump$tart National Standards / CEE National Standards, structured as
  ~15-minute modules per topic (financial decision-making, budgeting, income/careers,
  credit/borrowing, future planning) — a modular structure, not one long simulation.
- **FutureSmart is ESSA Level III validated** (as of March 2024) — independently shown to have
  a statistically significant positive relationship with students' financial knowledge,
  self-efficacy, and behavior. This is the evidence bar a facilitator-deployed learnership
  tool should eventually be measured against, not just "did it feel fun."
- Built on **game-based problem-solving + self-reflection activities** + real-world scenarios
  "designed to prime students for long-term behavioral change" — the self-reflection half is
  the piece HUSTLE is currently missing (see `decision-journal-and-feedback-loops.md`).

## General financial-gamification patterns (banking/fintech apps)

- Gamified financial-literacy modules report completion rates over 80% when scenario-based
  (make a decision, see the consequence immediately) rather than reading-based.
- Concrete mechanic examples from the fintech space: savings-streak badges, budget-mastery
  achievements for staying within a spending limit, investment-diversification milestones.
  **Greenlight** (kids' banking app) frames saving explicitly as "leveling up financial
  status" — accumulation-as-progress, not just a balance number.

## HUSTLE application

- HUSTLE's "real content extracted from the deployed bundle, not invented" discipline is
  already aligned with this genre's actual best practice (scenario-based, real consequences)
  — the critique's finding that consequences are hidden until the end is the gap versus this
  standard, not the scenario-writing itself.
- Consider whether HUSTLE should eventually seek the same kind of outcome validation EVERFI
  has (pre/post knowledge or self-efficacy measurement) once past prototype stage — this
  belongs in the ROADMAP as a "prove it works" milestone, not a Stage 1-4 feature.
- A **budget-mastery achievement** (from the fintech pattern above) is a cheap, concrete first
  version of the "Founder's Path" idea in `engagement-mechanics.md`: award it the first time a
  player survives a week without their opening capital dropping below a set floor.
