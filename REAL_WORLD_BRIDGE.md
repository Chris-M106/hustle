# HUSTLE — REAL-WORLD BRIDGE / BUSINESS EXECUTION LAYER

> Cross-reference, added 2026-08-08: HUSTLE is a mobile application, not a website — see
> `PRODUCT.md` → "Product target vs. current implementation" and `TESTING.md`. Nothing in this
> layer changes that; any future post-game/hypothesis UI this file's research leads to is
> subject to the same mobile-first design and real-rendered-app testing standard as everything
> else in the product.

The current world-class product-development process remains active and
should NOT be replaced.

Continue respecting the existing research, PRODUCT.md, ROADMAP.md,
DESIGN.md, current architecture, and all decisions already validated.

This is a NEW PRODUCT-DIRECTION LAYER that must be evaluated after the
current audit/implementation cycle.

============================================================
1. THE NEW QUESTION
============================================================

HUSTLE currently teaches the player how to make decisions inside a
compressed business simulation.

Now investigate a deeper product question:

"If I finish HUSTLE and actually want to start this business in real
life, do I understand what I would need to do next?"

The answer should be more than:

"I understand why I won or lost the simulation."

The stronger outcome is:

"I understand why my decisions mattered AND I have a practical path
toward trying this in the real world."

Do not assume the current product already achieves this.

Audit it.

============================================================
2. DO NOT TURN HUSTLE INTO A BUSINESS COURSE
============================================================

Do NOT respond to this problem by adding large amounts of educational
content, long explanations, checklists, or generic business theory.

HUSTLE must remain a game first.

The core experience remains:

PLAY
→ DECISION
→ CONSEQUENCE
→ EXPLANATION
→ REFLECTION
→ REPLAY

The new layer should extend that experience into:

SIMULATION
→ UNDERSTANDING
→ REAL-WORLD APPLICATION
→ ACTION

The player should never feel that the game has suddenly turned into
an online MBA course.

============================================================
3. DISTINGUISH THREE LEVELS
============================================================

Evaluate HUSTLE as three connected layers.

LEVEL 1 — THE GAME

Question:

"Can I make good entrepreneurial decisions?"

Examples:

- What business should I choose?
- Where should I operate?
- What should I spend my R2,500 on?
- Which supplier should I use?
- How much cash should I keep?
- How should I react to a crisis?
- Should I take a risk?
- How should I respond to customers?

This should remain fast, engaging and game-like.

------------------------------------------------------------

LEVEL 2 — BUSINESS BUILDER

Question:

"Can I turn this idea into a realistic business plan?"

Examples:

- Who is the customer?
- What problem am I solving?
- Who are the competitors?
- What will I sell?
- What will I charge?
- What will it cost?
- What equipment or stock do I need?
- Who will supply me?
- How will I get my first customers?
- How much cash do I need?
- What could go wrong?

This layer can be more deliberate and practical.

------------------------------------------------------------

LEVEL 3 — REAL-WORLD EXECUTION

Question:

"What do I actually do next?"

Examples:

- Talk to potential customers.
- Check competitor prices.
- Find suppliers.
- Compare supplier costs.
- Calculate startup costs.
- Determine a realistic price.
- Decide where to operate.
- Create a first offer.
- Find the first customer.
- Record the first transaction.
- Track costs and revenue.
- Review the first week's results.

This should become actionable rather than theoretical.

============================================================
4. RESEARCH THIS BEFORE DESIGNING IT
============================================================

Do NOT invent the real-world business journey from assumptions.

Research the actual sequence involved in starting and operating a small
business in the relevant target context.

Use credible and current sources where appropriate.

Research topics such as:

- validating a business idea
- customer discovery
- competitor research
- pricing
- startup costs
- cash-flow management
- suppliers
- inventory
- first customers
- basic bookkeeping
- separating personal and business money
- marketing
- operating a small business
- relevant South African requirements
- registration and compliance where applicable
- permits/licences where applicable
- tax considerations where applicable
- payment methods
- record keeping
- basic risk management

Do NOT turn every legal or administrative requirement into game
content.

Determine what belongs in:

GAME
BUSINESS BUILDER
REAL-WORLD ACTION PLAN
EXTERNAL RESOURCE / OFF-PLATFORM GUIDANCE

Clearly distinguish factual requirements from recommendations or
general advice.

============================================================
5. MAP THE REAL BUSINESS JOURNEY
============================================================

Construct a realistic high-level journey such as:

IDEA
→ CUSTOMER
→ PROBLEM
→ VALIDATION
→ COMPETITION
→ OFFER
→ PRICE
→ COSTS
→ SUPPLIERS
→ STARTUP CAPITAL
→ FIRST CUSTOMER
→ FIRST SALE
→ OPERATIONS
→ CASH FLOW
→ MEASUREMENT
→ IMPROVEMENT
→ GROWTH

Do not assume this exact sequence is correct.

Research and adapt it to the actual target audience and business types
represented in HUSTLE.

Identify which stages HUSTLE already teaches.

Identify which stages are missing.

Identify which stages should be simulated.

Identify which stages should become real-world actions.

============================================================
6. CONNECT GAME DECISIONS TO REAL LIFE
============================================================

Every important game mechanic should be evaluated for its real-world
counterpart.

Examples:

GAME:

"Keep R500 as a cash buffer."

REAL WORLD:

"Calculate your expected weekly expenses and decide how much cash you
need to keep available."

GAME:

"Supplier raises the price."

REAL WORLD:

"Find three suppliers and compare price, quality, minimum order and
reliability."

GAME:

"Customers are not buying."

REAL WORLD:

"Talk to potential customers and find out what they actually want."

GAME:

"Competition is high."

REAL WORLD:

"Visit or research competitors and identify what customers could get
from you that they cannot easily get elsewhere."

GAME:

"You need your first customer."

REAL WORLD:

"Identify ten potential customers and make your first offer."

The objective is NOT to make the game identical to real life.

The objective is to make the game teach concepts that transfer to
real-life decisions.

============================================================
7. THE POST-GAME BRIDGE
============================================================

Investigate a post-run experience:

"READY TO TRY IT FOR REAL?"

This should NOT simply be a congratulations screen.

It should transform what the player learned during the simulation into
a practical next-step plan.

For example:

YOUR HUSTLE
Phone Repair

WHAT YOU LEARNED
You survived because you kept cash available.

WHAT TO DO IN REAL LIFE

□ Talk to 10 potential customers
□ Check what local competitors charge
□ Find 3 suppliers
□ Price the equipment you need
□ Calculate your startup cost
□ Decide your first service
□ Set an initial price
□ Decide how you will get your first customer
□ Track your first sale

The exact structure must be researched and validated.

============================================================
8. PERSONALIZE THE REAL-WORLD PLAN
============================================================

The real-world layer should use information already generated by the
player.

Where appropriate, use:

- chosen business
- chosen location
- available capital
- strengths
- weaknesses
- decisions made
- mistakes
- successful decisions
- Field Notes
- crisis outcomes
- customer assumptions
- supplier decisions
- pricing decisions

Do not generate a generic entrepreneurship checklist.

The player should feel:

"This is MY business plan."

============================================================
9. FIELD NOTES → REAL ACTION
============================================================

Investigate how Field Notes can bridge the simulation and reality.

Example:

SIMULATION:

"You depended on one supplier."

FIELD NOTE:

"One supplier can become a single point of failure."

REAL-WORLD ACTION:

"Find two alternative suppliers before you commit your money."

This creates:

DECISION
→ CONSEQUENCE
→ LESSON
→ MEMORY
→ REAL ACTION

Evaluate whether this should become a core HUSTLE learning architecture.

============================================================
10. DON'T OVERPROMISE
============================================================

HUSTLE is not a replacement for:

- accountants
- lawyers
- government agencies
- business advisers
- formal education
- professional financial advice

Where real-world requirements vary by business, location or
circumstance, make that explicit.

Do not present generic guidance as a legal, tax or regulatory
requirement.

Where official requirements matter, point the user toward the
appropriate current official source.

============================================================
11. TARGET USER
============================================================

Keep the actual audience central:

A motivated person who may have limited formal business education,
limited prior exposure to entrepreneurship terminology, and limited
resources, but has a strong desire to succeed.

Do NOT make the real-world layer patronizing.

Do not reduce the intellectual challenge.

Use:

SIMPLE LANGUAGE
+
SERIOUS DECISIONS
+
PRACTICAL ACTIONS

Avoid unnecessary business jargon.

When business terminology matters, introduce it through the concrete
experience first and explain the term afterward.

============================================================
12. REAL-WORLD ACTION SHOULD BE SMALL
============================================================

Avoid overwhelming the player with a 50-item startup checklist.

Prioritize actions that can actually be completed.

For example:

TODAY
→ Talk to 3 potential customers.

THIS WEEK
→ Compare 3 competitors.

NEXT
→ Find 3 suppliers.

THEN
→ Calculate your startup cost.

The system should help convert ambition into action.

============================================================
13. SUCCESS CRITERION
============================================================

Add a new product success criterion:

After completing HUSTLE, a player should be able to answer:

1. What business am I trying to build?
2. Who is it for?
3. What problem does it solve?
4. What will I sell?
5. What will it cost me?
6. How will I make money?
7. What could kill the business?
8. What do I need to validate?
9. What should I do first in the real world?
10. What should I do next?

The player does NOT need to know everything.

They need to know enough to take the next intelligent step.

============================================================
FINAL PRINCIPLE
============================================================

HUSTLE should not merely teach:

"How do I survive a business simulation?"

It should teach:

"How do I think about starting a business?"

And then help the player answer:

"Now that I understand the basics, what is the next thing I can
actually do?"

The goal is:

PLAY
→ LEARN
→ UNDERSTAND
→ APPLY
→ ACT

Not:

PLAY
→ FINISH
→ RECEIVE A SCORE
→ LEAVE

Research first. Map the real-world journey. Identify the gap.
Do not implement until the product model is clear.
